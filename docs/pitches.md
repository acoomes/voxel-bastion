# Phase 0 pitches — Voxel Bastion

Three pitch lengths × multiple verb-anchored variants. For pitch validation use (5+ non-gamers, 5+ TD players). Companion to [`competitive-research.md`](competitive-research.md) and [`phase-0-hook.md`](phase-0-hook.md).

The research surfaced three patterns common to high-scale TD-adjacent comps: **single-image-legible hook**, **mechanically asymmetric phases**, **a verb the genre didn't already have**. Every pitch below leads with a verb that contains TD as a side effect — never "tower defense with X."

---

## How to use these for validation

Pick one variant per length, run the test, record reactions verbatim.

**Non-gamer test (recruit 5+):** read the 15-word, then ask: *"What kind of game does that sound like?"* You're listening for whether they reconstruct any version of "build defense + destroy world." If they ask "is it like Minecraft?" or "is it Tetris?" the pitch is too generic. If they say "huh, weird, tell me more" — that's the signal.

**TD-player test (recruit 5+ via Discord/Reddit):** read the 50-word, then ask *"what makes that different from Rogue Tower / Bloons / Kingdom Rush?"* You're listening for whether they immediately surface the destruction angle, or whether they pattern-match it to an existing comp. If they say "oh, like Rogue Tower then" you haven't differentiated.

**Both audiences:** at the end, ask *"on a 1–5 scale, how curious are you?"* and *"would you wishlist it on Steam?"* Track scores. <3.5 average is a signal to rework the verb, not the pitch length.

## 15-word elevator pitches

Pick the verb that lands hardest in conversation. Read the line, count to two, watch the face.

### A. Erode (recommended primary)

> A roguelite tower defense where every kill **erodes the battlefield** — your map shrinks as you fight.

Anchors on the screenshot moment that's unique to voxel destruction. "Erode" is a verb no comp owns. Single-image legible: a half-collapsed map mid-defense.

### B. Reshape

> A roguelite tower defense where towers and enemies both **carve through the world** they're fighting in.

Anchors on the two-sided destructibility — counters Dome Keeper's static-map criticism directly. Slightly less visceral than "erode."

### C. Sculpt

> A roguelite tower defense where you **sculpt the terrain into your defense** — no grid, no fixed paths.

Anchors on agency. Reads more like a building game; may pull TD players who bounced off rigid grids. Risk: drifts toward "sandbox," which is a different audience.

### D. Crumble

> A roguelite tower defense where the ground **crumbles out from under your towers** as the wave hits.

Anchors on tension/fragility. Strongest "oh shit" reaction per word. Risk: implies losing more than winning; may read as frustrating to non-gamers.

## 50-word short pitches

Lead with the verb. Show both phases (build / wave) inside the 50 words — that's the asymmetric-phase pattern from the comp research.

### A. Erode (recommended primary)

> Voxel Bastion is a roguelite tower defense where the battlefield erodes around you. Between waves you carve trenches and place towers; during waves the map shatters under boss explosions, enemies tunnel through your walls, and every successful defense leaves a different skyline. No two runs end with the same map.

### B. Two-sided destruction

> Voxel Bastion is a roguelite tower defense where both sides reshape the map. You dig high ground and channel enemies into kill zones; they tunnel through your walls and collapse the towers above. Each run authors its own arena. The screenshot at wave 24 is unique to that seed.

### C. Terrain-as-wall

> Voxel Bastion is a roguelite tower defense where the terrain *is* the wall. You don't place prefab barriers on a grid — you sculpt the ground itself into chokepoints, pillars, and overlooks. Then bosses blow holes in your work and enemies dig through it. Failure isn't a wipe; it's erosion.

### D. Hybrid (uses verb + comp anchor)

> Voxel Bastion is what Rogue Tower would look like if the map could deform. A roguelite tower defense where every shot leaves a mark — towers carve, bosses crater, enemies tunnel. Your defenses get reshaped by the fight you're fighting. Run length: ~30 minutes. Replay value: every map ends differently.

## 200-word long pitch

For the Steam page short description, devlog hook, press kit. Variant A is the recommended primary; B is an alternate framing if pitch testing surfaces "I don't get the destruction" reactions and we need to lean harder on the loop.

### A. Erode (recommended primary)

> Voxel Bastion is a roguelite tower defense where the battlefield erodes as you fight on it.
>
> Every run starts with a fresh procedurally-generated map, three acts of escalating waves, and a small defense budget. Between waves you do the usual TD work: place towers, plan kill zones, route the path. But the path isn't fixed. You carve trenches, raise pillars, and shape the terrain itself into your fortifications.
>
> Then the wave hits, and the map starts to come apart. Boss explosions blow craters in your work. Sapper enemies tunnel through your walls. Your own splash damage chips away at the terrain you were standing on. By the third act, the map you started with isn't the map you're defending anymore.
>
> Win, and you spend shards on permanent unlocks — new towers, modifiers, and starter perks for the next run. Lose, and you go again. Every seed produces a different skyline, and the screenshot at wave 24 is unique to that run.
>
> Roughly 30-minute runs. Permadeath. Solo. Built around the moment your tower falls into a hole.

### B. The loop (alternate framing)

> Voxel Bastion is a roguelite tower defense built around one rhythm: build, defend, watch the world come apart.
>
> Each run is a procedurally-generated map split into three acts. Between acts you face a shop and a vendor; during acts you face waves of enemies that don't just walk down a fixed path — they tunnel, dig, collapse, and reroute. Your towers do the same in reverse. Every shot leaves a mark on the terrain. Every kill changes the silhouette.
>
> Place towers, sculpt the ground, choose modifiers, push deeper. Die in the third act and earn shards toward permanent unlocks. Survive and unlock harder ascensions for the next run. The same seed twice produces the same map — the same player twice does not.
>
> No two runs look alike at wave 24, because no two runs erode the same way.
>
> Solo dev. Built on Three.js voxels. Free demo permanent on the Steam page. ~30 minutes per run.

## Notes on phrasing

- **Avoid** "polish," "innovative," "unique" — these are tells that the pitch doesn't have content.
- **Avoid** "tower defense reimagined / for the modern era" — Steam users have read this 800 times.
- **Use** the verb in the *first 5 words*. The research showed every successful comp does this; cinematic / setup-led intros under-convert at indie scale.
- **Use** specific numbers when possible: "wave 24," "30-minute runs," "three acts." Specifics convert better than abstractions.
- **Use** a comp anchor only if the comp player already knows the comp. The hybrid 50-word ("what Rogue Tower would look like…") is specifically for TD-savvy audiences; cut the comp anchor for non-gamer tests.

## Open question for the user

After validation, three things to bring back:

1. **Which verb won.** Did "erode," "reshape," "sculpt," or "crumble" get the strongest reaction?
2. **Comp the testers reached for.** What did they pattern-match it to? If everyone said "Dome Keeper" — fine, that's a known reference and the hook still differentiates. If they said "Minecraft" or "Bloons," the pitch leaked the wrong cue.
3. **The single confusion point.** One sentence per tester: *what was the part that didn't land?* That's the part to rewrite for the Steam page.

Bring those back and we'll lock the hook + finalize the wording for the Steam page short description.
